Owl Pellet ID

Identify the owl species from a pellet — and the prey remains inside — straight from a photograph.

How it works & honest limits: this tool is a heuristic identifier, not a deep-learning model. It combines measurable photo features (colour, gloss, shape, texture) with the details you give it (size, region, visible contents) and ranks them against a curated knowledge base. Treat results as a well-informed shortlist — confirm important IDs against a field guide or an expert.

    No photo? Use the guided key

    Open the step-by-step identification key
    1. Where was it found? Under a barn roof, church or derelict building → think barn owl. Woodland, parks or gardens → tawny. Open moorland or rough grassland → short-eared or harrier-like open-country owls. A winter roost with many pellets under one tree → long-eared.
    2. How big is it? Under ~30 mm → little owl / scops / screech / pygmy sized. 30–60 mm → barn, tawny or eared owls. Over ~80 mm → eagle-owl / great horned / powerful sized.
    3. Colour and sheen (fresh pellets): black and glossy/varnished → classic barn owl. Grey-brown, matt, loosely felted → tawny. Small, grey, with obvious beetle cases → little owl.
    4. Contents: almost entirely small-mammal fur and bones → barn owl. Mammals plus birds, worms and insects → tawny. Dominated by beetle and insect remains → little owl. Large bones, rabbit or bird remains → eagle-owl / great horned owl.

    All analysis happens in your browser — photos are never uploaded anywhere. Knowledge base: data/owls.js & data/prey.js. Contributions welcome.
